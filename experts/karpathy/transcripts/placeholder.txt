Hi everyone, so today I want to show you how neural networks actually work
under the hood, and we're going to build it all from scratch. The thing I
really want you to walk away with is the intuition, not just the equations. So
let's start with the most basic building block, which is a single value, and
we're going to wrap it in this little object I'll call Value.

The whole game of training a neural network comes down to one idea:
backpropagation. Backpropagation is just the chain rule from calculus, applied
recursively backwards through a graph of operations. Every time we do an
addition or a multiplication, we build up a little graph, and each node knows
how to send the gradient backwards to its inputs.

So what's a derivative, really? People get intimidated by it, but it's just:
if I nudge this input by a tiny amount, how much does the output respond? That
sensitivity is the gradient. And once you have the gradient of the loss with
respect to every weight, training is almost embarrassingly simple — you just
nudge each weight a little bit in the direction that decreases the loss. That's
gradient descent.

A neuron itself is nothing fancy. You take a bunch of inputs, multiply each by
a weight, add a bias, and then squash it through a nonlinearity like tanh. Stack
a few of those into layers, stack a few layers into a multilayer perceptron,
and that's a neural net. The magic isn't in the architecture, it's in the
training loop: forward pass, compute the loss, backward pass, update. Over and
over. That's really all there is to it.
